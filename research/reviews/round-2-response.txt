R2-01 — Paired physical denominators and CSV completeness
The original JSON already included local-interruption burn and CO2, but the CSV exports omitted them. Added localAdditionalBurnT and localAdditionalCo2T to results.csv and sensitivity.csv. The existing additionalBurnT/additionalCo2T columns describe the amount required to deliver the specified net quantity; the local columns describe the quantity actually consumed using the bounded extra-origin headroom.

At four hours, k=0.035/hour, linear retention:
- One tonne NET DELIVERED: 1.162790697674 tonnes extra origin uplift, 0.162790697674 tonnes burn, 0.514418604651 tonnes combustion CO2.
- One tonne EXTRA ORIGIN HEADROOM, with a one-tonne destination gap: 0.86 tonnes displaced local uplift, 0.14 tonnes burn, 0.4424 tonnes combustion CO2, 0.14 tonnes unfilled gap.
These are different denominators and cannot be combined as one outcome. The README now states this explicitly. A new test checks both CSVs' export logic, fuel conservation, combustion-factor consistency and this specific four-hour distinction. All 522 exported cases have independently checked local burn and CO2.

R2-02 — Availability of the counterfactual
The economic comparison assumes that the ordinary destination purchase is available. The separate local-interruption calculation asks how much of a specified uplift gap can be displaced if the origin supplies extra fuel. It does not assign an avoided purchase cost to an unavailable transaction, estimate disruption welfare or demonstrate flight continuation. README language now separates these cases. A shared regional quota remains an additional-burn accounting identity rather than a prediction of cancellations or optimal emergency allocation.
